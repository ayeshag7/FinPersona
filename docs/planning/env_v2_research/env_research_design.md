## 0. What the current generator is (baseline for every comparison)

From `envs/synthetic_market.py` (read in full):

- **Price formation: exogenous, scripted.** Phase lengths are hard-coded as ⌊0.4T⌋/⌊0.3T⌋/remainder. Bull trap: V is a 3-segment GBM-like path; P tracks V+N(0,1) in phase 1, then `P = P_exit + cumsum(N(1.5,0.5))` in phase 2 and `+cumsum(N(0.5,2.5))` in phase 3 — V does not enter the price after τ1 (audit D-3). Crash: `P = 0.92·V + N(0,1.5)` applied over **all** days, with a 0.98·V ceiling from τ1 (audit D-2: no panic onset). Flat: V is GBM (μ=0.0005, σ=0.02), `P = V + N(0,0.5)·φ_t` with `φ_t = max(0.5, 0.7φ_{t-1} + 0.3|ε_t|)` where ε_t is an independent draw, not the return innovation (audit D-8: no ARCH feedback, |r| autocorrelation ≈0 at all lags).
- **Fundamental: exogenous, hidden in name only.** `earnings_per_share = V/15`, `reported_PE = P/EPS` ⇒ `V = 15·P/PE` exactly (audit F-1: max reconstruction error 0.0000; `sign(P−V) == sign(PE−15)` on 100% of steps). `dividend_yield = 0.4·EPS/P` is a second exact channel.
- **Observables (16 fields; only 10 reach the prompt, audit D-6):** price, SMA20, "SMA60" (actually 50-day), RSI14, MACD, volume, volume_ratio, news_sentiment (i.i.d. N(0,0.3), phase-shifted mean), sentiment_MA5, sentiment_change, implied_volatility (deterministic linspace clock, identical across seeds — audit F-8), reported_PE, dividend_yield, trend_strength, trend_regime.
- **Accounting (`portfolio_tracker.py`):** fractional shares (`shares = budget/price`), no commission, no spread, no slippage, execution at the observed same-day price, long-only, no leverage, daily decisions, mark-to-market every step.
- **Validation performed: none** (no stylized-facts test, no leakage probe, no baseline policies, no resolvability analysis). Audit F-2: in the flat scenario 96.9% of steps have |P−V|/V < 1%, so the "objectively correct action" is inside the generator noise.

Everything below is organised around closing those gaps with documented precedent.

Reading of the table: every LLM-market environment with an explicit fundamental (Lopez-Lira, SSW replications, Machine Spirits, Hashimoto, del Rio-Chanona) defines it **analytically from a dividend/discount structure or a stochastic process** and validates either (a) stylized facts against real data (Hashimoto, TwinMarket) or (b) theory-consistent behaviour and mispricing metrics against human experiments (Machine Spirits, SSW). None of them uses hard-coded phase scripts; none of them leaks V through an accounting identity. FinPersona's niche — an exogenous, seed-reproducible, **phase-controlled** decoupling with a referee-side V — is not occupied by any of them, but only if the decoupling is real and audited.

Cross-cutting (from the survey of these models): every classic ABM keeps the fundamental **exogenous and simple** — constant (Day–Huang, Chiarella 1992, BH98, GH, FW, Chiarella–Iori, LtFE), log random walk (Lux–Marchesi, Farmer–Joshi), or AR(1) dividends (SFI-ASM); decoupling arises from chartist extrapolation plus a switching/herding/threshold mechanism and is bounded by a stabilising force (fundamentalist demand, misalignment penalty α_p or exp(−x²/α), threshold exits). Only Franke–Westerhoff 2012 validate with a formal moment-matching test and report pass rates. Hommes 2006 (Handbook of Computational Economics vol. 2, 1109–1186, doi:10.1016/S1574-0021(05)02023-X) is the single best citation for the equations above.

Pass/fail is usually "sign and order of magnitude within the empirical band", not a formal test; we should do both (report the statistic with CI across seeds and a Ljung–Box p-value).

### 2.2 Pros and cons for an LLM benchmark

**Exogenous scripted (current):**
- + Seed-reproducible regimes; a referee-side V that is independent of agent actions (single agent cannot move price, so V is never a function of its own trades); cheap; phases are controllable factors for a factorial design.
- − Phases are hard-coded day indices ⇒ time and phase are perfectly confounded (§6); realism defects (no volatility clustering, clock-like IV/volume); reviewers (nhWU N3) see unjustified constants; the mispricing dynamics have no theoretical provenance.

**Endogenous ABM (FW/LM/BH/FCN):**
- + Bubbles/crashes emerge from calibrated behavioural demand; stylized facts come for free with published parameterisations (FW 2012 DCA-HPM); citation-defensible.
- − Regimes are stochastic: a given seed may or may not produce a bubble in a 200-day window (FW mean chartist share ≈0.17; bubbles are episodic). To guarantee "bull trap at seed s" you must select seeds (= a new form of scripting) or run thousands of days. The agent's own orders would have to enter the market-maker equation (otherwise it is still an exogenous process in disguise); with a single LLM agent that makes V-independence and reproducibility fragile (ABIDES' careful per-agent PRNG design exists precisely because of this). Much harder to cross with personas × models × seeds in a fixed token budget.

### 2.3 Recommendation: the hybrid

**Keep the price exogenous to the LLM agent (no price impact), but generate the mispricing by a calibrated fundamentalist–chartist process with scripted, randomised regime triggers.**

Concretely (a "FW-HPM-on-V" generator):

1. **Fundamental** V_t: GBM (drift μ_V, σ_V) as now, plus optional scripted fundamental events (a negative drift shock for the crash). Cite as GBM, not Black–Scholes (audit D-9).
2. **Mispricing** x_t = log P_t − log V_t evolves by the FW market-maker equation: `x_{t+1} = x_t + μ(n^f_t φ(−x_t) + n^c_t χ(x_t − x_{t−1})) + σ(n) ε_t`, with `n^f_t = 1/(1+exp(−β a_t))`, `a_t = α₀ + α_n(n^f−n^c) + α_p x_t²`. Default to FW DCA-HPM estimates (φ=0.12, χ=1.50, σ_f=0.758, σ_c=2.087, α₀=−0.327, α_n=1.79, α_p=18.43, μ=0.01) scaled to daily units.
3. **Regime triggers (the scripted part):** a regime variable s_t ∈ {calm, mania, panic} drawn from a 2–3-state Markov chain (Hamilton 1989) whose onset day and duration are seeded and logged; in "mania" α₀ is shifted toward chartism (or an exogenous positive predisposition shock is injected); in "panic" a fundamental shock plus chartist dominance. Because the switch is a seeded latent state, you retain controlled regimes per seed, but onset/duration vary (§6), and within a regime the path is FW-generated rather than a linear drift. Calibration anchors for the chain exist (Ang & Timmermann 2012: monthly S&P high-vol/low-vol P=0.977, Q=0.951; Ang & Bekaert 2002 bear-regime duration ≈6.9 months) — but for a 200-day benchmark the durations should be chosen for the design (e.g., mean event duration 60 days) and *stated as design choices with the empirical anchor quoted*, rather than claimed as estimates.
4. **Volatility of P** is then endogenous to x_t and regime (FW generates clustering); drop the decorative IV clock and report IV as a noisy function of realised |r| (or drop the field).
5. **Guarantee of scenario validity** by **rejection sampling on the referee side** with the criterion logged (e.g., bull trap: max_t x_t ≥ +0.25 within the episode; crash: min_t x_t ≤ −0.10 after the fundamental shock). Report the acceptance rate per scenario so reviewers see how "forced" the regime is — this is the honest replacement for the inert "structural invariants" (audit F-10).

This keeps (i) seed-reproducibility, (ii) V independent of agent actions, (iii) phases as an experimental factor, and adds (iv) published provenance for the decoupling dynamics and (v) stylized facts testable against Cont 2001. It is strictly cheaper than an order-book port (ABIDES/PAMS), which the audit already recommends against.

If the team prefers the minimal path, the fallback is "scripted but stochastic": keep the three-phase script, randomise onset/duration (§6), fix the V→observable leak (§3), add real GARCH(1,1) feedback (Bollerslev 1986 with α+β≈0.95), and publish stylized-fact tables. The hybrid is recommended because it answers nhWU N3 ("unjustified choices") with an estimated model rather than hand constants.

## 3. Leakage (ground-truth recoverability) audit protocol

### 3.1 Precedents

- **KTD-Fin §4.3 / Table 12**: attack the *exact payload the agent sees* with ≥10 independent models; report top-1/top-5 recovery, a strict joint criterion, random baseline and Wilson CIs; threshold implicit = "within a small additive margin of the random baseline" (top-1 ≤3.0% vs 0.3% random; joint ≤1.5%).
- **Machine Spirits §3.4**: (i) keyword search over rationales for memorised content; (ii) direct elicitation of the hidden structure from the system prompt alone; a model is flagged if it recovers the experiment/result; the key quantity (bubble height) was not recovered by any model.
- **Partial-input baselines** (Gururangan et al. 2018, NAACL; Poliak et al. 2018, *SEM): a cheap model on partial input vs majority class (≈67% vs 34% on SNLI) exposes artifacts; Feng, Wallace & Boyd-Graber 2019 (ACL) warn that *failure* of such a baseline does not certify absence of artifacts. [arXiv 1803.02324](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02324), [S18-2023](https://aclanthology.org/S18-2023/), [arXiv 1905.05778](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05778).
- **Probe design**: Hewitt & Liang 2019 control tasks / selectivity (probe accuracy minus control) [arXiv 1909.03368](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368); leakage as "unexpectedly strong feature–target predictiveness" (Kaufman et al. 2012, ACM TKDD, doi:10.1145/2382577.2382579); MI estimators (MINE; KSG fails at d≳20) and Bayes-optimal classifier log-loss as MI proxy (Gupta et al. 2024, [arXiv 2401.14283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14283)).
- **Finance look-ahead audits**: Glasserman & Lin 2023 anonymised headlines [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322); HindsightBench four-arm date manipulation [arXiv 2607.18867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867); "When Alpha Disappears" one-switch leakage gain [arXiv 2605.23959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23959); survey MR-5 requires "explicit leakage vectors checked (feature fitting, text hindsight, label leakage)". No finance benchmark reports an R² threshold for a hidden target; we would be first, so state the threshold as a design choice and justify it against the resolvability band.

### 3.2 Protocol for FinPersona (four layers, all on the *rendered prompt fields*, not the internal DataFrame)

| Layer | Test | Statistic | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 algebraic | Symbolic/regression check that no closed-form combination of shown fields equals V (the current EPS=V/15 identity fails this) | Exact-reconstruction max error over all steps/seeds; currently 0.0000 | max |V̂−V|/V > generator noise floor on ≥99% of steps (i.e., no exact identity) |
| L2 statistical (surrogate) | Fit (a) OLS/ridge, (b) gradient-boosted trees, (c) small MLP from the contemporaneous observation vector O_t (and from the last k=5 observations) to **log(P_t/V_t)** (the decision-relevant target) and to V_t; train on seeds 1–N/2, test on held-out seeds and held-out scenarios | Out-of-sample R², and sign accuracy of sign(P−V) on *resolvable* steps (|P−V|/V ≥ θ) | **R²_oos(log P/V) ≤ 0.30 and sign accuracy ≤ 0.70** for any surrogate; compare against (i) "P-only" control (price & technicals only, no valuation fields) and (ii) a chance control with shuffled V — report selectivity = R²(full) − R²(P-only). Rationale: the task must remain *statistically inferable* (otherwise the "fundamentalist" mandate is unplayable) but not *algebraically recoverable*; 0.30/0.70 sits well below the current 1.00/100% and above chance (0 / 0.5). Report the numbers; the threshold is a pre-registered design choice. |
| L3 model-side (KTD/Machine-Spirits style) | Feed the exact rendered prompt (persona removed) to ≥5 frontier LLMs as attackers: "estimate the fundamental value / say whether the asset is over- or under-valued and by how much" | MAPE of V̂; sign accuracy vs chance; also keyword audit of rationales for "fair P/E of 15" style priors | LLM sign accuracy ≤ surrogate sign accuracy + 5 pp; MAPE ≥ 10%; report Wilson 95% CIs (200 probes stratified by scenario × phase × seed, as in KTD-Fin) |
| L4 resolvability | Fraction of steps with |P−V|/V ≥ θ where θ = c·σ_noise,t (derived from the generator, audit A2) | Coverage per scenario/phase | Report; require ≥ 60% coverage in event phases and state flat-scenario coverage honestly (currently 3.1%) |

Practical notes: randomise the fair-value multiple per run (not 15); make EPS a lagged, quarterly-stale, noisy accounting estimate (audit B1 sketch) so that P/E is informative but not invertible; run L2 every time the generator or observation space changes (CI test); publish the table as a paper artifact (MR-5, MR-6).

## 4. Baseline policies to compute inside the environment

Precedent: CLQT computes 8 passive baselines automatically every run and reports "beat /8" (§6.2); TradingAgents / FinAgent / InvestorBench / StockBench / Agent Trading Arena report B&H, SMA, MACD, ZMR, equal-weight ([2412.20138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138), [2402.18485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18485), [2412.18174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18174), [2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209), [2502.17967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17967)); OpenPM adds Random-N (10 seeds) and always-flat cash ([2608.09988](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09988)); FINSABER shows B&H beating LLM agents over 2004–2024 once costs are applied ([2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)); Lopez-Lira uses deterministic always-buy/sell/hold + gap/MR/momentum agents as controls; "Drift No More" scores drift as KL to a goal-consistent reference policy (audit §06). The audit's own run (F-3) shows why this is mandatory here: "BUY day 1 then HOLD" scores RG 99.7% in the bull trap, always-HOLD 79.8%, random 66.9%, bracketing both reported arms (85.9 / 78.4).

Minimum suite (all run per scenario × seed, zero API cost, every metric reported for each):

1. always-HOLD (100% cash), always-BUY, always-SELL, random (1/3 each, 10 seeds), BUY-day-1-then-HOLD (buy-and-hold), constant-mix at each C_ideal ∈ {0.2, 0.5, 1.0} with daily rebalancing (the *mandate-perfect* reference; gives the MAS floor, audit F-5), momentum (SMA20>SMA50 ⇒ BUY else SELL), mean-reversion (RSI<30 BUY / >70 SELL), **V-oracle** (BUY iff P<V(1−θ), SELL iff P>V(1+θ), else HOLD — the RG ceiling), and a **mandate-conditional oracle** (V-oracle constrained to the persona's cash target band).
2. Normalisation: report each agent metric as (agent − trivial floor)/(oracle − trivial floor) where the floor is the best of {always-HOLD, random, buy-and-hold} and the ceiling is the (mandate-conditional) oracle; also report raw. Report "beats k/9 baselines" CLQT-style.
3. Reliability artefacts: log parse failures / API fallbacks as a separate reliability rate and exclude fallback HOLDs from behavioural metrics (CLQT §7.11: 17/17 autonomous HOLD rounds were scaffold failures; survey MR-6 immutable logs).

## 5. Cost / friction model recommendation

Field numbers: FinRL default `transaction_cost_pct = 0.001` per side, integer shares ([docs](https://finrl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/Introduction/SingleStockTrading.html)); Qlib `open_cost 0.0005 / close_cost 0.0015 / min_cost 5` ([docs](https://qlib.readthedocs.io/en/stable/component/workflow.html)); Zipline `$0.001/share` commission, `VolumeShareSlippage(0.025, 0.1)` or `FixedBasisPointsSlippage(5 bps)`, integer-share rounding; Backtrader default commission 0, fractional only via `CommInfoFractional`; vectorbt defaults fees 0 with fractional sizing. Empirical: Frazzini–Israel–Moskowitz live costs ≈ 4.5 bps impact vs VWAP, median ≈6 bps per rebalance for large caps ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs)); Novy-Marx & Velikov 2016 effective spreads 20–57 bps/trade for mid-turnover anomalies (RFS 29:104, doi:10.1093/rfs/hhv063). CLQT LOW tier = 2.0 spread + 0.5 commission + 1.0 slippage bps with square-root impact, validated against paper fills; OpenPM 22 bps/$ traded with cost-multiplier sensitivity {0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2}×. Survey MR-4: report "execution timing/price assumption, rebalancing frequency, fees/spread/slippage/impact model … plus sensitivity analysis under alternative frictions"; the survey finds only 1 of 19 closed-loop studies modelled costs.

Recommendation for a behavioural (not profitability) benchmark:
- **Default tier "low"**: proportional cost 5 bps per side (≈ CLQT low total 3.5 bps + rounding to a round number), applied as a deduction from cash on BUY and proceeds on SELL; **execution at next-day open** (or same-day close with the decision made on the prior close — state it; MR-3 "price formation e.g. next-open/close/mid"); no market impact (single agent, exogenous price — state explicitly that impact is out of scope by design).
- **Sensitivity tiers** {0, 5, 20 bps} reported for the headline metrics (CLQT/OpenPM pattern). Zero-cost remains the legacy comparator.
- **Fractional shares: keep**, but say so (vectorbt precedent; Zipline/FinRL do not) — it avoids integer-lot artefacts at $10k capital and matches the "percentage of cash" action space; note in MR-3.
- **Decision frequency: daily**, one decision per bar, long-only, no leverage, no shorting — document as the I/O contract (MR-3), and log per-step turnover so the flat-scenario "churn" failure mode has an economic cost (audit §06: zero cost makes churn free).

## 6. Time-vs-phase identification

Problem: with τ₁=⌊0.4T⌋ fixed, "day 100" and "mania" are the same event across all seeds, so any temporal effect (MSD) is unidentifiable from a phase effect (the audit's F-7 null model and reviewer Q-seg).

Precedents for separating them:
- **Random change-point locations**: `ruptures.pw_constant` draws breakpoints with Dirichlet-distributed segment lengths per seed ([ruptures](https://github.com/deepcharles/ruptures/blob/master/src/ruptures/utils/drawbkps.py); Truong, Oudre & Vayatis 2020 [arXiv 1801.00718](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00718)).
- **Non-stationary RL**: piecewise-stationary tasks with "regime switches at randomized intervals (Poisson-distributed around T_switch), preventing trivial schedule memorization", K=4 regimes, 10 seeds ([arXiv 2605.16170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16170)).
- **Markov-switching**: Hamilton 1989 (Econometrica 57:357) gives geometric durations with mean 1/(1−p_ii); Ang & Bekaert 2002 (RFS 15:1137) simulate regime-switching paths to evaluate allocation rules; CTMSTOU uses continuous-time Markov switching with random holding times in an ABM ([arXiv 2202.00941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00941)).
- **Experimental economics**: SSW 1988 fixed 15 periods confounds time and phase; indefinite-horizon / random-termination designs were introduced to break it (Duffy et al.; [link](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~duffy/papers/Indefinite_Horizon_Asset_Market.pdf)).
- **Arike et al.**: the instrumental→evaluation transition occurs at a pre-determined step that does not depend on agent actions, and phase lengths I ∈ {2,4,8,16,32} are varied as a factor (App. A.7–A.8) — the closest LLM-agent precedent to "vary onset as a factor".
- **Machine Spirits**: fixed 50 periods, but bubble timing metrics (T_bottom→peak, HALF) are measured relative to the bubble start, not to calendar time — the analysis-side remedy.

Design for FinPersona (combine three devices):
1. **Randomised onset and duration per seed**: τ₁ ~ U(0.25T, 0.55T), τ₂ ~ U(0.2T, 0.4T) (or Markov-chain draws), logged in metadata; analyses use *phase-relative time* (days since onset) as well as calendar time, and fit `metric ~ phase + calendar_day + (1|seed)` so the two are separable (mixed-effects, as the audit's A6).
2. **Mirrored orderings**: include event-first episodes (panic/mania beginning at day ≈10 then resolution) alongside setup-first episodes, so "late" is not always "event". Keep the scripted-regime labels in the referee log.
3. **Phase-free long horizons**: the flat scenario (and a "flat-then-flat" T=800) is the pure time control; report temporal drift there first — if MSD is real it must appear without any regime.
4. Pre-register that headline temporal claims are computed on windowed (not expanding-minimum) statistics and tested against a constant-exposure null (audit F-7).

### Honest statement of FinPersona's environment novelty (for the paper)
- **Not novel**: synthetic single-asset market with an exogenous fundamental (ABIDES oracle, PAMS/Hashimoto GBM fundamental, Lopez-Lira dividend fundamental all predate it); stylized-facts validation (Hashimoto Table 3, TwinMarket Table 4); leakage probes (KTD-Fin, Machine Spirits); cost tiers and baselines (CLQT).
- **Novel, if delivered**: (i) a referee-scored, *phase-controlled* price–value decoupling whose regimes are randomised in onset/duration and generated by a calibrated fundamentalist–chartist mispricing process rather than an order book — giving reproducible, agent-independent ground truth at a per-step granularity that LOB simulators cannot provide for a single LLM agent; (ii) a published leakage/resolvability audit with thresholds on the rendered observation; (iii) mandate-conditional oracle and trivial-policy normalisation of behavioural metrics. That is a measurement-instrument contribution, and it should be claimed as such (the audit's "instrument before mechanism" framing), not as market realism.
