# Synthetic environment v2: what is wrong

Consolidated list from three independent review agents (A: parameter provenance, B: claims versus evidence, C: methodology and robustness), 23 Aug 2026, plus the team lead's own four concerns. The full reviews are in `docs/env_v2/reviews/`. Items marked **[verified]** were re-run by me after the reviews and confirmed; the rest are reviewer findings with file references that can be checked the same way.

Severity: **H** = invalidates a headline claim or the benchmark's measurement; **M** = weakens a claim or biases a result; **L** = tidy-up.

---

## Part 1. The seven things that matter most

1. **[verified] [H] The fixed start price is a hidden answer key.** Every run starts at price 100 and value 100 (`envs/v2/generator.py:63,188`). Because value drifts slowly, log(P/100) is the mispricing to within about 0.08 for the whole run. A two-line rule "sell to band-high if price > 105, buy to band-low if price < 95" reaches regret 0.005 (bull trap), 0.006 (crash), 0.013 (flat) against the true-value oracle's 0.003 and always-hold's 0.10 to 0.14. It only fails in the sustained bull (0.098), which is exactly the scenario where our published observables-oracle gap is largest. Every "price history predicts mispricing by design" statement, the L2 audit, the L5 oracle, the strict xfail in CI and slides 23 to 25 are explained by this anchor, not by "smooth value and persistent mispricing". Fix: draw the start price at random per seed and per asset (log-uniform), rescale earnings and analyst fields, re-run every audit with a level-free price control.

2. **[verified] [H] The Franke-Westerhoff model is not what runs.** The misalignment term uses `price_scale = 100` (`envs/v2/mispricing.py:52,167`), which saturates the switching: the fundamentalist share is above 0.99 on 98 % of days (mean 0.998). The chartist parameters have no effect on any path; the calm process is an AR(1) with a constant weight. The `ar1` engine reproduces the checklist item for item. Slide 6 and the script present a fundamentalist/chartist model as a design contribution. The spec itself flagged the units as "to be verified against the FW 2012 PDF" and it never was. The SMM "not identified" result was computed inside this saturated regime. Fix: verify the units against FW 2012, set the scale accordingly, re-derive phi and re-run; or present the calm process honestly as AR(1) plus GARCH.

3. **[H] With the anchor removed, the fields do leak.** Reviewer C re-ran the L2 audit with the start price randomised: price-only R2 of mispricing falls to 0.22 and the full field set gives 0.78, so the non-price fields add 0.56 of R2 (we publish +0.11). The channels are deterministic functions of the hidden state: P/E with a multiple drawn from a range as narrow as the mispricing itself; sentiment whose mean is 0.6 tanh(2x); volume that rises with |x|; the analyst estimate built as V times an error. "Valuation fields no longer pin down the value" does not survive a level-free audit. Fix: widen or time-vary the P/E multiple; make sentiment respond to returns, not to x; drive volume by |returns| and turnover; re-test.

4. **[verified] [H] The flat "control" market is biased cheap.** Rare jumps (mean −4 %) enter the mispricing, not the value, so the flat market's mispricing averages −0.10 (median −0.07, 70 % of days negative, day-1 mean −0.08). The oracle says "buy" on three quarters of resolvable flat steps, and a persona that sits at its band-low edge beats always-hold. Any "buy the dip" persona effect is confounded with this undocumented generator bias. Fix: put jumps in the value process or make them mean-zero in x; add a test that E[x] is about zero in flat.

5. **[H] The claim that the hidden value is withheld is contradicted by our own audit.** The 50-seed audit recovers V to 3.5 to 4.9 % MAPE and x with R2 0.90 to 0.96 from the shown fields; the pre-registered L2 gate fails; the calibration report says "the level of V is recoverable to about 4 %". Slide 25 says "the environment withholds the level of value". The slide's "no formula reproduces V, at least 12 % off" is the median error of price itself, i.e. the median size of the mispricing, and the candidate set is four linear formulas; a three-term average already gets 9 % median with 27 % of days within 5 %. Fix: state the L2 FAIL and the 4 % MAPE on the slides; extend the L1 candidate set; report error percentiles, not medians.

6. **[H] The calendar is still a phase clock in the runs we actually do.** All LLM runs use the setup-first ordering and render "Day-N". In that population P(calm | day <= 50) = 1.00 and P(event | day >= 170) = 1.00; day-only phase accuracy is 80 to 87 %. The 65 % on slide 22 comes from a mixed set of orderings that no LLM run uses. So "time versus phase" is not identified in the grid as designed, which was the original v1 complaint. Fix: put event-first and phase-free orderings in the grid, widen the setup range, and stop rendering the absolute day (also randomise the quarter phase of `days_since_eps_announcement`).

7. **[H] "Nothing re-tuned to pass" is not true.** Amendments A1, A2 and A6 changed the statistic or the criterion of items 7, 9 and L1 after a fail, and each change turned a fail into a pass or margin; alpha, gamma, beta, sigma-bar, the jump rate, the panic multiplier, phi and the bubble hazard were all tuned against the checklist (eleven variants); several checklist bands (items 9, 11, 13, 17, 20) were moved to what the generator produces. Fix: replace the tile with the list of amendments and tuned parameters, and show the pre-amendment results beside the amended ones.

## Part 2. Numbers with no adequate justification

The rule the team lead set: every number must come from published finance literature, then be fitted and stress-tested. Reviewer A classified about 200 numeric choices; roughly a dozen trace to a published statistic (FW 2012 index parameters, Tetlock 2007 sentiment coefficients, 5 bp cost, the practitioner cash bands, Wilder/MACD windows). The rest are plan anchors without a source, calibration choices, or arbitrary. The ones that matter, with what would justify them:

8. [H] `sigma_V = 0.006`/day (value volatility). Cited only qualitatively. Decides how recoverable V is from price averages. Justify from the volatility of a smoothed fundamental proxy (Vuolteenaho 2002 cash-flow news; quarterly EPS x constant from Compustat); sweep 0.004 to 0.012 and report L2, L4, item 20.
9. [L] `mu_V = 0.00025`/day (6.5 %/yr drift). Plan anchor; cite Dimson-Marsh-Staunton or CRSP price-only return and show insensitivity.
10. [H] Mispricing half-life 150 d (phi 0.463). The one parameter that was supposed to be estimated is a hand constant; the docs give four values (60 to 120, 90, 120, 150). Justify from firm-level reversion of log(P / V_hat) on CRSP/Compustat (Poterba-Summers 1988; Balvers-Wu-Gilliland 2000); sweep {30, 60, 150, 300, 580} through the checklist and the LLM grid.
11. [H] `price_scale = 100` in FW (see item 2).
12. [H] GJR-GARCH alpha 0.10, gamma 0.10, beta 0.83, sigma-bar 0.017, t df 5, panic multiplier 5. Every value moved off the plan's index-level anchor to raise checklist pass rates. Fit GJR-GARCH-t per stock on 100+ CRSP large caps 2000-2024; use the median and bracket with quartiles; run the quartile sets through the LLM grid.
13. [M] Jump rate 0.010/day, jump size N(−4 %, 3 %). Raised from the plan's 0.004 to 0.008 to 0.010 for the fat-tail item; the shipped configuration was never run in the published variant table. Fit a jump-diffusion or count |r| > 4 sigma days in CRSP.
14. [M] Phase variance multipliers (deterioration 1.5, panic 5, stabilisation 1.5, mania 1.5, blow-off 2, post-top 3). No source; they are label-tied and are what the IV clock reads. Anchor on regime-conditional volatility (Ang-Timmermann 2012; Greenwood-Shleifer-You 2019).
15. [M] Event ranges: setup 25 to 55 % of horizon, deterioration 15 to 40 d (unanchored), panic 15 to 70 d, depth 10 to 30 %, discounts 55/70/85 %, recovery U(delta, 1), 30-day ramp, front-loading shape. All design choices with loose secondary anchors. Tabulate peak-to-trough duration and depth from Mishkin & White 2002, Barro & Ursua 2017, Greenwood-Shleifer-You 2019 and either show the ranges cover the empirical interquartile range or sample from it.
16. [H] Error-correction gains 0.10 / 0.05 / 0.10 that force x onto the scripted path. No literature can exist for a tracking gain; they decide how much of an event is script and how much is process. Report the variance share of event-phase moves explained by the script; sweep 0.02 to 0.25.
17. [H] Bubble hazard (h0 3e-4, b 6, drift cap 0.012) and its targets (40 to 60 % topped, peak P/V 1.6 to 2.5). The topped target is a 2-year industry statistic applied to 150 days of one stock; the P/V band has no source; the calibration score weights are arbitrary. Fit b from Greenwood-Shleifer-You Table 4; derive h0 from the horizon; fit the mania drift with LPPLS so the cap is not needed.
18. [H] Sustained-bull forcing (lambda 0.15) and validity band [−0.10, 0.15]. No possible literature; 40 % of draws are rejected against a band with no source, leaving a selected quiet sub-population.
19. [M] Sustained-bull drift 0.15 to 0.25 %/day (35 to 65 % in 200 days). Back-solved to an unsourced return band. Use the 200-day returns of non-crashing run-ups in GSY 2019 or top-decile CRSP winners.
20. [H] Hidden P/E multiple U(14, 22). Far narrower than the real cross-section (P10 to P90 about 10 to 35), which is why P/E recovers V to 15 % median. Use the Compustat cross-section; sweep width (listed as a sensitivity in the plan, never run).
21. [H] Analyst error sd 0.15, rho 0.95. Unsourced and optimistic: target-price errors are 30 to 40 % sd (Brav & Lehavy 2003; Bradshaw-Brown-Huang 2013). Also implemented at 0.34 by a bug (item 44).
22. [M] Earnings noise 10 %, reporting lag 25 to 35 d, payout 0.35, dividend stickiness 0.7 (Lintner's annual speed applied per quarter). Cite Compustat dispersion, RDQ lags, payout distribution; convert Lintner to quarterly.
23. [H] Sentiment model: mean 0.6 tanh(2x) + 0.3 tanh(ret20/0.15), AR 0.85, noise 0.25. No source for any constant; sentiment reads x directly. Tetlock 2007 is sentiment-to-returns, not sentiment-to-mispricing. Fit on daily news sentiment (RavenPack / Tetlock) against returns and valuation.
24. [M] Volume model: AR 0.65, +0.25 |r|, +1.2 |x|, noise 0.30. The |x| loading has no source (Karpoff-type evidence is volume-to-|r|). Fit on CRSP daily volume.
25. [M] Implied volatility: 21-day horizon, premium 0.20, stress premium 0.35 above the top decile of the path's variance, floor 12. Premium form and levels unsourced; the decile trigger is a code-level choice. Use single-stock VRP from OptionMetrics.
26. [H] Resolvability threshold theta = 0.05 (with 0.03 / 0.08). "Stated, not derived"; it defines what a correct action is, the oracle's switching, the regret score and coverage. Derive from the observable-implied error in x or a cost break-even; sweep through every headline metric.
27. [L] Band half-width 0.10; dead band 1 pt; 5 bp per trade (the anchor is a round trip, implemented per trade); temperature 0.2. State or cite (Donohue-Yip 2003 for rebalancing bands).
28. [M] Stateful arms: window 20 turns, 60k token budget, summary every 10 turns with 5 raw. "Cheapest design"; the decay thesis is a function of context length. Pre-register window length as a swept factor.
29. [M] Multi-asset: volatility scales [1, 1, 0.5], common-factor correlation 0.3. No provenance (typical pairwise stock correlation 0.3 to 0.5; low-vol decile ratio 0.7 to 0.8).
30. [M] Seeds and horizon: 50 seeds at 200 days (checklist), 20 seeds at 800 days (persistence item), 150 of 400 paths after subsampling (leakage audit), 60 seeds (hazard), 12 train / 10 eval seeds (observables oracle), 25 seeds (sensitivities), 5 seeds x 3 replicates (grid default). No power analysis anywhere; four checklist items sit within one standard error of their threshold; the promised 200-seed run was not done.
31. [M] Checklist thresholds themselves (80 % shares, kurtosis 1.5, ACF bands, IV bands, MDD bands, rejection < 5 %). Set in the plan without an empirical reference distribution; several later moved. Replace each with the P10 to P90 of the same statistic on real 200-day windows of large-cap stocks (CRSP; OptionMetrics for IV).
32. [H] Leakage thresholds: L1 noise floor 1 % (hard-coded), L2 absolute (R2 0.30 / 0.90, MAPE 10 %) inconsistent with the design, L2b margin 10 pp frozen after the result was known. Derive the price-only bound analytically from sigma_V and the half-life and gate on selectivity relative to it, with a label-permutation null.
33. [M] Cash bands 0.70 to 0.90 / 0.40 to 0.60 / 0.00 to 0.20. Best-sourced numbers in the repo, but the Merton arithmetic uses index inputs (premium 4.3 to 6 %, sigma 16 to 20 %) while the environment is a single stock with about 28 % volatility and pays no dividends into cash; with the environment's own parameters the "balanced" gamma lands in the conservative band. The JFE ordering evidence does not transfer to MBTI personas (our own research note, section 1.4). State the bands as practitioner conventions and derive an environment-consistent version as a sensitivity; pay dividends or remove the field.
34. [L] Undocumented constants found only in code: `min_resolution = 10`, `STAB_RAMP_DAYS = 30`, `SENT_SD_REF = 0.35`, `PE_CAP = 200`, module hazard fallbacks (0.0015, 6.0) that differ from the calibrated 0.0003, IV top-decile trigger, trader band (0.4, 0.6) in the runner versus (0, 1) elsewhere, sleeve mask 0.05, random-baseline seeds, all surrogate hyperparameters, two different random-forest specifications for the one pre-registered surrogate, stateful chars/4 token estimate and 160-char truncation, calibration score weights and ticker list.

## Part 3. Claims that are stronger than the evidence

35. [H] "Return moments do not identify the persistence" rests on one Nelder-Mead run (150 iterations, likely stalled), ten hand-picked surviving mega-caps, a one-dimensional phi profile computed at parameters where chartists never act, and a proxy weight matrix instead of the bootstrap one; the tool also normalised the innovation weight after simulating, so the rejected fit was on a mis-scaled model. Defensible version: multi-start global optimisation, full-parameter profiles with the bootstrap weights, moments that carry persistence (variance ratios at 20 to 250 days, P/E or price-to-SMA250 autocorrelation), 100+ stocks including delisted ones, several sub-periods, and a chi-square acceptance rule.
36. [H] "Stationary half-life 150 d, about 70 d realised on a 200-day path". The 72 d comes from 20 seeds at 800 days; on the benchmark's own 200-day windows the same estimator gives 14 d. Reviewer B showed the 72 d is estimator bias: a pure AR(1) with a true 150-day half-life gives a median sample estimate of 62 d at T = 800 and only 54 % of paths clear the 60-day floor, and the same estimator gives 84 to 96 d for a process whose true half-life is 580 d. Item 9's pass is a coin flip per path and cannot tell 150 from 580. Report the analytic half-life plus a bias-corrected estimate at 200 seeds for T in {200, 800, 2000, 5000}; also the pilot-implied 187 d disagrees with the stated 150.
37. [M] "Clustering, fat tails and crash asymmetry are now properties of the series". Items 2, 3 and 6 fail. And the "structural" explanation is wrong in the other direction: reviewer B applied the same criteria to 310 real 200-day windows of ten US stocks and got Ljung-Box rejection in 29 % (we get 60 %), kurtosis > 1.5 in 63 % (we get 79 %), median ACF of |r| 0.09 (we get 0.15). The criterion is mis-specified for 200-day windows; the generator is, if anything, more clustered than real stocks at that horizon. Re-derive items 2 and 3 from the empirical distribution; report per scenario and on calm windows only, because the regime-switching panic variance inflates pooled kurtosis and |r| autocorrelation.
38. [M] "Checklist pass counts are stable across calibration variants". The calibration-target items are exactly the ones that move (60-day half-life fails items 9 and 11 with 32 % topped; index parameters halve the item-10 R2), and margin items flip at random at 25 seeds. Report per-item statistics with confidence intervals at 50+ seeds, and include the `ar1` engine.
39. [H] Sensitivity pass/fail counts on slide 25 and in the calibration report are wrong in five of six rows (the generated files' footers disagree with their own tables: index 8/7 not 7/6; omega 7/8 not 6/7; panic x3 9/6 not 8/5; panic x6 8/7 not 7/6; Pruna 7/8 not 6/7). **[verified]**
40. [M] "Severity now matters". Item 10 fails both statistics (partial R2 0.38 vs 0.7; spread 18 vs 20 pp), and the R2 criterion is unattainable by construction of the statistic (a three-level factor against MDD with about 9 pp of panic noise). Say "delta moves mean MDD by 18 pp but explains 38 % of its variance"; pre-register an effect-size criterion.
41. [M] Bubble item 11 shown as PASS while being a calibration target (hazard grid-searched to it); item 17 (40 % rejection versus 5 %) labelled "calibration" in the tile. The "without a cap every run reaches P/V > 3" claim was never tested (no uncapped row in the hazard grid).
42. [M] "About 40 % of draws are rejected and the rate is published" understates the consequence: the accepted sustained-bull paths have daily volatility 0.0147 versus 0.0240 for the rejected ones, negative return autocorrelation, and are recalled at 82 % from price alone. The control is a selected sample and a different process (item 18).
43. [M] "Within 0.02 to 0.08 regret of the true-value oracle, so the game is playable". Computed with 10 evaluation seeds, no confidence intervals, an attacker trained across seeds on five lags of all fields that exploits the start-price anchor; a stateless agent seeing one day's fields has none of that. With level-free features, sign accuracy on resolvable steps is about 0.71 rather than 0.95.
44. [M] Pilot sentences such as "the directive placebo tracks the static arm, which says it is the content of the reminder" are causal readings of one model, one seed, one replicate; the pilot also covered three scenarios, not four as the script says.
45. [L] Statements about the plan's anchors ("the plan's setting", "typical of US equities") are in several places my paraphrase of a design choice rather than a citation in the plan; the script's "Where the numbers come from" lines for GARCH coefficients, the 21-day IV horizon, the event ranges and the 5 bp cost should be re-written once real sources exist.

## Part 4. Design and methodology flaws

46. [H] Implied volatility is a one-day phase marker: the phase multiplier enters the conditional variance and the 21-day forecast deterministically (`garch.py:69-111`), so log-IV jumps by a factor 1.9 on the first panic day (z about 7 against calm day-to-day noise). The 4-class L2b accuracy cannot see a change-point leak. Also the stress premium threshold is a quantile over the whole path including the future (`observables.py:152`): look-ahead. Fix: ramp the multiplier through omega or build IV from rolling realised variance plus noise; expanding-window threshold; add an onset-detection audit.
47. [M] In event phases x is a scripted target plus noise, not the "published form"; the mania drift sits at its cap on 38 % of mania days, so the bubble is exponential rather than super-exponential for most of its life.
48. [M] Within a 200-day run the mispricing is close to a constant offset (200-day sd 0.053, half-life 14 d as measured), so the oracle's target changes zero or one times per run and 77 to 91 % of resolvable steps sit at one band edge. The benchmark measures a one-shot "which side of the band" call fixed by the day-1 mispricing, not judgement over time. Report oracle switches per run; reconsider theta relative to within-run variation, or the horizon.
49. [M] The blow-off label is a calendar label (last third of the mania) in the 58 % of bull runs that never top; the recorded bubble top is one step early.
50. [M] Burn-in of 260 days is under two half-lives for the default engine and under half a half-life for the index-parameter sensitivity, so that sensitivity never reaches its stationary state.
51. [M] Rejection sampling redraws the whole schedule per attempt, and the hazard was calibrated with a penalty on rejections rather than on realism.
52. [M/H] The regret score (MCR) is mostly band adherence with a one-bit direction call attached; it tracks band-MAS almost one-for-one in the pilot. Decompose it into band violation and within-band directional agreement.
53. [H] MCR normalisation is mislabelled: the code's ceiling for a lower-is-better metric is the constant-mix baseline, not the mandate oracle (`metrics_v2.py:166-198` vs `tools/report_v2.py:130` and the pilot notes), so the published "norm_mcr" values mean "as good as constant mix".
54. [H] The day-1 separability gate is ill-posed under the start-at-target design: `report_v2.py:116-117` feeds the change in cash share into a gate that checks ordering and band membership of a level, so a persona-consistent agent that stays at its centre fails it (pilot band-hit 0.0). Models would be excluded on a gate that cannot pass.
55. [M] Salience shares (persona vs start vs directive) are not identified under start-at-target because the three are collinear; the promised bootstrap intervals are not implemented.
56. [M] Baselines are rebuilt from run metadata without n_assets, engine, b_pred or env_config, so for any non-default cell the agent and its floor/ceiling are on different paths.
57. [M] Stateful memory arm: the "turns since mandate" covariate ignores the re-injected block (it is about zero, not what is logged), the history contains twenty copies of the mandate, and parse fallbacks are stored as the model's own prior turn.
58. [M] Multi-asset: all assets share one scripted event, so cross-asset structure is script plus noise (pairwise x correlation 0.46 to 0.65); the "defensive" asset has the same crash and bubble with half the noise; the existence-rule oracle rewards correlated noise; the IV premium is inconsistent across assets; all assets start at 100.
59. [M] Statistics: the mixed model nests seeds inside models although seeds are crossed with models, uses random intercepts only although random slopes were pre-registered; run-level bootstraps ignore clustering by model and seed; multiplicity is corrected only across seven metrics within a cell, not across the roughly 840 contrasts; the circular-shift null has seven distinct values per run.
60. [L] Execution timing: under next-open execution the logged cash share is pre-trade, penalising that sensitivity by a day. Placebo length matching is asserted, not tested.

## Part 5. Testing and robustness gaps

61. [M] No power analysis for any checklist item; several items are decided by fewer than a standard error; sensitivities at 25 seeds cannot distinguish variants; the leakage audit subsamples 150 of 400 paths; the observables oracle has 10 evaluation seeds and no intervals; the persistence item uses 20 seeds.
62. [M] Longer horizons were used only where the 200-day result failed (item 9, amendment A2) and never as a systematic study; the properties the agent experiences in 200 days (14-day measured half-life, 0.053 sd) are never reported as such.
63. [M] Not-like-for-like before/after rows on the slides: half-life (v1 on 200-day windows, n = 100, vs v2 at 800 days, n = 20); phase-from-calendar "100 % to 65 %" pairs v1 within-scenario with v2 mixed-set (mixed-set is 63.6 % to 64.8 %, i.e. unchanged; within-scenario is 100 % to 73.6 %); "R2 0.90 to 0.83" pairs the v1 full-feature model with a stale 20-seed v2 price-only number (50-seed v2 is 0.90 full / 0.79 price-only); the drawdown spread compares a 10-pp delta range in v1 with a 30-pp range in v2.
64. [M] Checklist criteria that were written but never implemented: item 12's "lagged correlation equals configured b_pred" and item 13's "non-degenerate across seeds".
65. [M] The generator is not frozen: no hash test covers `envs/v2/*.py` or `params/*.json`, and the provenance code hash covers only the facade file, so a change to the GARCH, event or mispricing code leaves the recorded hash unchanged.
66. [M] Tests are structural (shapes, ranges, reproducibility) rather than statistical; nothing asserts E[x] about zero, the analyst error sd, IV continuity, or the fundamentalist-share distribution; the CI leakage test is a strict xfail, so a green CI now requires the leak; two legacy tests are broken.
67. [L] Pre-registered pieces missing from code: held-out-scenario L2 split, the L3 LLM probe, bootstrap intervals on salience shares, random slopes, the JFE-spread comparison (`targets.py:35` defines it, nothing uses it).

## What a robust v3 would need, in order

1. Randomise the start price; make the L2/L5 price control level-free; re-run every audit and rewrite the leakage story from the results.
2. Fix the FW units (or rename the engine), fix the analyst-error bug, move jumps to the value process, put the sustained bull on the same mispricing process as the flat market; freeze the generator with hashes over `envs/v2/**`.
3. Replace each plan anchor with a fitted estimate from a stated dataset (CRSP/Compustat/OptionMetrics or public equivalents) with a published interquartile range; list the ones that cannot be fitted as design values and sweep them.
4. Rebuild the checklist thresholds from the same statistics on real 200-day windows, with a power analysis per item and 200+ seeds; report at 200 days as "what the agent experiences" and at longer horizons separately.
5. Run the event-first and phase-free orderings in the grid and stop rendering the absolute day.
6. Repair the metric layer (regret decomposition, normalisation labels, the day-1 gate, baselines per cell) and the mixed-model specification.
7. Redo the SMM properly (multi-start, persistence-carrying moments, 100+ stocks, several periods) before saying anything about identification.
8. Run generator-parameter sensitivities through the LLM grid, not only through the checklist, for the six parameters that drive the results (sigma_V, half-life, theta, P/E range, analyst sd, sentiment loading).
