### 4.2 E1.2 — the three-estimator decomposition of σ_V, s_x, h

**Estimator A (variance ratios; `e1_2/vr_fit.md`).** Pooled Lo–MacKinlay VR(k), k ∈ {5, …, 500}, set A full sample (n = 417),
weighted minimum distance in (σ_V, s_x, h); intervals from a stock bootstrap (1,000) and a joint stock × 250-day block
bootstrap (500). σ_V = 0.0205/day [0.0197, 0.0213] (stocks) / [0.0167, 0.0243] (blocks); s_x = 0.024 [0.022, 0.026];
h = 5 d [4, 6] / [1, 13]; random-walk share of daily variance 0.74; J = 80 on 8 moments (the one-component model is
rejected). Sub-periods: σ_V 0.014 (2013–19) to 0.026 (2008–12); h 1–17 d.

**Estimator B (log(P/V̂) AR(1), Andrews median-unbiased; `e1_2/pv_fit.md`).** EDGAR quarterly EPS de-duplicated on
(concept, start, end) keeping the earliest filing, V̂ = EPS_ttm × median trailing P/E (sector where ≥ 5 names), monthly
2009-06..2024-12, ≥ 60 valid months; n = 367 stocks, 19.5 % undefined months (negative or zero EPS_ttm). h = 256 d [223, 277]
(OLS 167 d: the median-unbiased correction adds ≈ 90 d); s_x = 0.39 [0.36, 0.44]; the four concept × multiple variants agree
within their intervals (h 256–294 d). σ_V from Δlog V̂ (0.034) is overstated by the measurement noise in V̂ and is not used.

**The two disagree by a factor of 50 in h and 16 in s_x.** An exploratory two-component fit on the VR curve (NOT
pre-registered; `e1_2/vr_two_component_exploratory.md`) shows why the curve alone cannot settle it: a second transitory
component is strongly preferred (J 8.6 vs 84 for one component), the curve prefers a half-life of ≈ 40 d for it, and
forcing h₂ = 256 d (estimator B) or 150 d (the engine) fits worse (J 18.6 / 15.5) while moving σ_V to 0.016 / 0.018 and
s_x to 0.19 / 0.13 — over a 500-day horizon a one-year component is nearly a random walk, so σ_V and the slow component's
variance trade off. Under every fit σ_V is 2.7–3.4 × v2's stipulated 0.006.

**The recovery study (REG-5; `e1_2/recovery.md`, `recovery_diagnostics.json`).** Synthetic panels of 100 stocks ×
6,300 days from the equivalence-checked calm simulator with known h ∈ {5, 10, 30, 60, 120, 150, 250, 500} d × σ_V ∈
{0.006, 0.012}, V̂ = V·e^m with the FIT noise (sd 0.569) at persistence {0, 0.9}/quarter; A and B at 200 replications
per cell, C at 50 (the pre-registered counts; the h = 5 and 10 cells were added post-review — ADDENDUM §6.1 — because
both A and C locate the data below the original grid). Usability: median |ĥ − h|/h < 0.20 and, for A and B, interval
coverage ≥ 0.90.

- **A** is never usable: its point estimate is fair at h = 5 (0.11) but degrades monotonically with h, and its
  stock-bootstrap interval covers the truth in 0–1 % of replications — the intervals are far too narrow. Diagnostic
  (not pre-registered): on an *exact* random-walk + AR(1) panel A recovers h = 30 and 150 (ĥ 30.4, 147), so the
  estimator is sound and the engine's mispricing simply is not the AR(1) its mapping assumes.
- **B** returns ĥ ≈ 36–47 d whatever the truth (and ≈ 343–475 d when the V̂ error is persistent), including on a panel
  with **x ≡ 0**: at the panel's noise level (sd 0.57 against s_x ≈ 0.13) the monthly AR(1) of log(P/V̂) measures the

- **C is usable for h ≤ 150** (0.03–0.16) and unusable for h ≥ 250 — a coherent limit: over a 6,300-day panel a
  half-life beyond a year is close to a unit root and the variance-ratio/ACF moments cannot pin it. At the cell where
  the data actually sits (h ≈ 5, nearest σ_V cell 0.012) C's error is **0.05** with ĥ = 5 against a truth of 5.

[0.01892, 0.02036]**/day, s_x = **0.129 [0.125, 0.135]**, h = **4.82 d [4.17, 5.42]**, J = 66.6 on 9 moments.
**[Phase 2 correction, 1 Sep 2026]** The s_x figure is wrong: `e1_2_recovery.smm_fit` returns `s_x = sbar/0.017 x 0.1752`, the calm engine's stationary sd(x) at the REFERENCE half-life of 150 d, applied whatever half-life the fit returned. **Decision by the pre-registered rules (`e1_2/decision.md`): C is the only usable estimator, so C is adopted alone.**
Two qualifications are carried with the adoption, both stated rather than resolved: (i) the recovery panels are drawn

pull rate (CAL, from the v2 pilot).

**Application status (P1-15).** s_x_fit and h_fit are Phase-2 targets, not generator parameters, and are written to
`value.json` now. σ_V *is* a generator parameter: writing 0.0196 changes every path, which triggers the plan's
execution-order rule — E1.4's jump placement, E1.5's burn-in, E1.1's start-price test and this phase's whole after-state
were measured at 0.006. It is therefore recorded as **adopted, not yet applied**, with the value in force stated, and
the scope decision (re-run the cascade now, or fold the change into Phase 2's engine re-fit, where σ_V and the pull rate
are jointly identified) is put to the team in §6.

### 4.5 E1.1 — the start-price answer key (REG-1; D13) — `e1_1/start_price.md`

Four mechanisms behind one switch (`GenConfig.start_price_mode`): `fixed` (v2: V₁ = P₁ = 100), **A** `randomise` (P₁
drawn log-uniformly from the FIT range 7.47–240.02, V₁ = P₁·e^(−x₁)), **B** `normalise` (P₁ ≡ 100, V₁ = 100·e^(−x₁); the
provisional default, D13), **C** `both` (B plus a per-seed render scale k applied to every price-denominated rendered
field: price, SMAs, MACD, analyst fair value). 500 seeds × 4 scenarios per mechanism (seeds 50000–50499, generated under
the state in force: x_zero jumps, 750-day burn-in), the GBT attacker for x with the level set (51 features), the
level-free set (45) and the full set (111), GroupKFold(5) by path, Δ = R²(level) − R²(level-free) per scenario with a
500-resample cluster bootstrap; **rule: the CI's lower limit ≤ 0 in every scenario.** Rule-100 test: the MCR of "cash →
band-high if log(P_rendered/100) > θ, band-low if < −θ" (ISFJ, θ 0.05, PortfolioV2 at 5 bp) beside the oracle, always-hold,
constant-mix and the two constant band-edge policies; **rule: inside the union of the edge policies' intervals** (a rule
that is *better* than both edges is reading V).

(The sustained-bull R² are negative for every set — the pooled attacker does not transfer to that scenario's late-path

Readings. (i) v2's answer key reproduces exactly as Phase 0 found it: with V₁ = P₁ = 100 the level set reaches
R²(x) 0.76–0.90 and a one-line rule sits within 0.01 of the oracle. (ii) **B, the provisional default, fails both rules**:
with P₁ ≡ 100 the rendered level encodes the cumulative return since day 1, log(P_t/100) = (V_t − V₁) + (x_t − x₁), and
because V drifts little over 200 days that is x_t − x₁ up to noise — for an AR(1)-like x with a 150-day half-life the
implied R² for x_t is (1 − ρ^t)/2 ≈ 0.1 at day 50 and ≈ 0.3 at day 200; the attacker finds 0.08–0.21 (largest in crash,
where the post-crash level is far from 100), and the "compare with 100" rule beats both band edges in every scenario
(MCR 0.045–0.084 against edges 0.05–0.15) — not the oracle's 0.003, but an information channel the plan's design intended
to close. (iii) A and C pass both rules: a random start level (with or without the render scale) carries no information.
(iv) The field channel (reported, Phase 5's): `k·analyst_fair_value` inverts V with a median APE of 0.10 under fixed/A/B
(within 5 %: 26 % of steps) — the analyst field remains the strongest single candidate; under C the same candidate has
median APE 0.70 because the per-seed render scale defeats a single fitted k (by design). `k·P` itself has median APE 0.085
(that is |x|'s own scale).

**Decision.** Per the pre-registration ("if B fails the attacker rule, that is reported as a failure and the team is

### 4.6 E1.3 — the σ_V × s_x sweep with the Kalman bound (`e1_3/sweep.md`; 200 seeds per point, local reference environment)

Grid σ_V ∈ {0.004, 0.006, 0.010, 0.015, 0.020} × df_V ∈ {Gaussian, t₅} × s_x ∈ {0.10, 0.13, 0.165, 0.20} (the engine's
s̄ scaled so the realised sd(x) at T = 5,000 equals the target: 0.101 / 0.130 / 0.163 / 0.197), 200 seeds × 4 scenarios
per point (seeds 60000–60199), the level-free price-only surrogate (GBT, GroupKFold by path) for x with 500-resample
cluster-bootstrap intervals, and the analytic Kalman bound (Appendix B) beside it.

**Environment caveat (ADDENDUM §6.2).** The first run of this sweep was executed on Kaggle and reported calm R²
0.11–0.44 with gaps of +0.08 to +0.34 over the bound. That table is **not reproducible**: the same code and seeds on the
local reference machine give 0.153 where Kaggle gave 0.407 (and 0.066 vs 0.305, 0.152 vs 0.376 — `e1_3/repro_point.md`),
on a panel proven identical (same 800 paths, 95,863 calm rows, 45 features, identical coverage, half-life and sd(x)).
The cause is **not identified**: thread count and `early_stopping='auto'` were tested and refuted as mechanisms
(bit-identical R² at 1 and 4 threads, `e1_3/determinism_check.md`), and the SEP before-audit *does* reproduce across the
same two machines (0.385 vs 0.383), so the failure is regime-dependent rather than a blanket property of the estimator.
The table below is the **local** run; the Kaggle table is kept as `e1_3/sweep_kaggle.md`, record-only, and every
conclusion that rested on it is withdrawn.

- **The surrogate does not exceed the analytic bound.** Gaps span −0.175 to +0.042 across the 40 points, and **0 of 40**
  have a 95 % interval lying entirely above the bound. The largest positive gaps (+0.034 to +0.042) are at small s_x
  (0.10–0.13) with σ_V 0.006–0.010; at large σ_V with small s_x the surrogate is *below* zero (−0.11, −0.15: returns
  carry almost no information about x there). Appendix B's bound is therefore an adequate description of what a
  level-free price reader can extract from this generator, and the Phase-1 report's earlier claim of a flagged
  non-price channel at every grid point does not survive the environment correction. What Phase 6 must still fix is the
  **estimator environment** (pinned library versions) as part of any gate definition; the bound itself is usable.
- **σ_V trades the level-free R² against V's own variance**, as the plan expected but more weakly than the Kaggle table
  suggested: at s_x = 0.165 (t₅) the calm R² falls from 0.162 (σ_V 0.004) through 0.153 (0.006) and 0.145 (0.010) to
  0.066 (0.020), while MAPE(V) of a 250-day SMA rises from 0.129 to 0.194.
- **The D3 consequence table.** Coverage of |x| ≥ θ is a property of s_x, not σ_V (θ = 0.05: 0.62 at s_x 0.10 → 0.80 at
  0.20, unchanged across σ_V); item 9's half-life is 66–73 d at every point (failing mostly at s_x = 0.10); and item 20's
  calm-day sd (rule 0.014–0.022) is **0.0135 at the σ_V in force (fails low), 0.0158–0.0195 at σ_V 0.010–0.015 (passes)
  and 0.0236 at 0.020 (fails high)** at s_x = 0.165 — so the checklist's own magnitude rule prefers a σ_V *between* the
  stipulated 0.006 and the fitted 0.020, which is evidence the team should weigh in D3 alongside E1.2's estimates.
- df_V: t₅ and Gaussian differ by ≤ 0.02 in calm R² and by nothing in coverage or persistence — the DESIGN choice (t₅ in
  force) has no consequence at this stage; Phase 3's tail re-fit owns it.
- μ_V nuisance (50 seeds at the parameters in force): calm R² 0.152–0.169 against a bound of 0.153, with no trend across
  μ_V ∈ {0, 0.00025, 0.0005} — μ_V does not leak.

### 4.7 E1.6 — the level-free leakage audits, L5 and the checklist, before and after

**Design.** Every row below is on the standard evaluation panel (SEP: 1,600 paths, 320,000 rows, no subsampling), with

reported: the **level-free** control (returns, ratios to moving averages, RSI, MACD/P, trend fields — the Phase-1
default) and the v2 **level** control (price, SMAs, MACD in price units), kept for the record. No gate is applied.

**What the four rows say.**

1. **The start-price answer key is closed.** Before, the level control reached calm R²(x) = 0.811 against 0.383 for the
   level-free reader: the fixed start price was worth **+0.43 of R²** on its own (weakness items 1, 3, 43, now with
   intervals). After, the same contrast is 0.161 against 0.086 — **+0.075** — so about five-sixths of the level channel
   is gone. The residual is expected and not a defect of the mechanism: a per-seed render scale removes the level's
   meaning *across* paths, but within one path the trajectory relative to its own first day is still visible, and the
   level-free control does not include every long-lag ratio.
2. **Inferring V got substantially harder.** The full-field reader's log V R² falls 0.892 → 0.529 and its MAPE(V) rises
   4.1 % → 12.5 %; the level-free reader's rises 9.7 % → 15.7 %. This is mechanism C's per-seed scale doing exactly what
   E1.1 predicted.
3. **The analyst field is no longer an inverter.** L1: `k·analyst_fair_value` median APE 0.0996 before (within 1 % on
   5.6 % of steps) → **0.672** after (within 1 % on 0.7 %); `k·P` 0.115 → 0.126. Under a single fitted k the analyst
   field was the strongest single route to V; the render scale defeats it. What remains for Phase 5 is the *within-path*
   channel, which no start-price mechanism can close.
4. **The level control was flattering the phase clock.** L2b macro-class selectivity: before, +8.6 pp with the level
   control (**passes** the 10 pp margin) but +16.4 pp level-free (fails); after, +12.4 pp with the level control and
   +11.7 pp level-free (both fail). The
   v2 control passed only because the price level itself carried the phase, inflating the price-only baseline — which is
   the clearest single argument for the level-free control becoming the default. Registered for Phase 6 (P1-11).
5. **The absolute v2 gates still fail, before and after** (calm R² 0.928 → 0.823 against ≤ 0.30; event 0.971 → 0.944
   against < 0.90; MAPE(V) 4.5 % → 12.2 % against ≥ 10 %, the only component that now passes). Reported, not gated: the
   dominant remaining channel is the non-price fields (full set 0.823 calm against the level-free 0.086), which is
   Phase 5's subject, and Phase 6 derives the gates.

## 6. Decisions the team must take

1. **D3 — σ_V, h, s_x (E1.2) — answered by the rules; the application is the open question.** The full-scale
   recovery study (all 32 cells, A/B at 200 and C at 50 replications, grid extended to h = 5 and 10 where the data sits)
   makes estimator **C the only usable estimator**, so by the pre-registration C is adopted: **σ_V = 0.01957
   [0.01892, 0.02036], h = 4.82 d [4.17, 5.42], s_x = 0.129 [0.125, 0.135]** **[Phase 2 correction: s_x = 0.0250; §4.2]** (§4.2). h_fit and s_x_fit are already in
   `value.json` as Phase-2 targets. **σ_V is not yet applied**, and the team must choose how to apply it:
   - **(a) Apply now and re-run the cascade.** σ_V changes every path, so the execution-order rule requires re-running
     E1.4 (jump placement), E1.5 (burn-in), E1.1 (start price) and the entire after-state at σ_V = 0.0196 before the
     state can be handed over as audited. Cost: roughly a further day of compute on the present machines.
   - **(b) Record it and let Phase 2 apply it.** σ_V and the engine's pull rate are jointly identified — the recovery
     study's own finding is that the engine's mispricing is not the AR(1) the estimators assume, and Phase 2's E2.4
     chooses the engine and E2.6 sweeps persistence. Applying σ_V once, inside that re-fit, avoids paying the cascade
     twice. Phase 1 then hands over a coherent state at σ_V = 0.006 with the fitted value recorded and labelled.
   - Evidence for weighing them: A and C agree on the magnitude (0.0205 and 0.0196, 3.3 × the value in force) by
     independent routes; the checklist's own calm-σ rule (item 20, band 0.014–0.022) is met at σ_V ≈ 0.010–0.015 and
     fails at both 0.006 (0.0135, low) and 0.020 (0.0236, high) — so the fitted value does not sit comfortably inside
     the checklist band either (§4.6); and the adoption carries the two qualifications in §4.2 (the recovery design
     favours C; J = 66.6 shows misspecification on the real panel).
   My reading, offered as a recommendation and not a decision: **(b)**, because the cascade would be paid on a σ_V that
   Phase 2 may itself move once the engine is re-fitted, and because nothing in Phases 2–6 is invalidated by carrying a
   documented, labelled mismatch — whereas re-running now fixes the environment to a value whose own diagnostic (J) says
   the model is wrong.

2. **D13 — the start-price mechanism (E1.1) — taken.** Provisional B failed both pre-registered rules; A and C
   passed; the team chose C on 30 Aug 2026 (§4.5, ADDENDUM §6, P1-13). Still open for Phase 9: A vs C on the naturalness
   of the displayed level (REG-1's LLM test).
3. **The engine's residual E[x] ≈ +0.012 in flat markets** (E1.4 confirmatory and jumps-off runs): registered as a
   known defect owned by Phase 2; the team decides whether a residual of a third of the smallest θ is acceptable until
   the engine re-fit.
4. **Ratification of the two criterion corrections** (ADDENDUM §2, §4): the E[x] TOST at 1,000 seeds and the burn-in
   KS bound at 2,000 paths, each derived after the pre-registered form proved undecidable, with disclosure of what had
   been seen.
5. **REG-15 triggers.** Every tail statistic here is survivor-based (set A); the crash-depth and jump-share values
   understate the universe. No parameter was set from them alone, but Phase 3's size re-fit (E3.2) will be — the WRDS
   re-run (D1) is the remedy the plan foresees.
6. **The estimator environment (E1.3, E1.6).** The GBT surrogate's R² is not portable between library environments
   (0.15 local vs 0.41 on Kaggle for the same code, seeds and panel — ADDENDUM §6.2, `e1_3/repro_point.md`), while the
   SEP audit on the same two machines reproduces to three decimals (0.383 vs 0.385). Thread count and the estimator's
   `early_stopping='auto'` were tested and refuted as causes (`e1_3/determinism_check.md`); the mechanism is still
   unidentified and is handed to Phase 6 as an open item. Everything surrogate-based was regenerated on the local
   reference environment. Phase 6 must pin the library versions in the gate definition and re-test reproducibility **on
   the panel the gate will use** — one reproducing reference row is not evidence for another, as this pair shows.
   With the local table the analytic Kalman bound is *not* exceeded anywhere (0 of 40 grid points significantly above),
   so the bound remains usable as Appendix B intends.
