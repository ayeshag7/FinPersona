## 2. The six open questions (as decision inputs)

**Q1 — Is the architecture as specified in Eq. 3/5, or does the implementation pass state the spec omits?**
Both defects at once. The implementation passes **portfolio state** (cash + holdings value) in every user prompt — omitted from Table 2 and Eq. 3; that is where App L's "$6,585.57" comes from. It passes **no history whatsoever**. And beyond Cycle 1's account: the static arm never contains the Core Mandate at all (V1b) — the equations are actually right about this and the prose is wrong. Decision consequence: the formal spec is incomplete (portfolio state) **and** the mechanism claim is untestable (no accumulating context) **and** the "re-grounding" framing is a misnomer (nothing to re-ground). This is not documentation-level; it invalidates the paper's central construct as framed. It is *not* fatal to the data — the runs are internally consistent measurements of a different, honestly-describable manipulation (instruction present vs absent in a stateless prompt).

**Q2 — Newer draft?** No. Byte-identical text extracts, no other version in git or docs/. Review targets the right artifact. (The revision plan, conversely, targets an *older* draft — E24.)

**Q3 — Classifier training data?** Days 1–5 rationales from all conditions in the passed results directory, **both arms included** (no Agent_Type filter in code); 2,372 train / 418 val rows, val split random within-run (not held-out models/arms); model list not recorded in metadata. The P(intended persona) memory-vs-static contrast is therefore contaminated by mandate-vocabulary echo. Every classifier-based number (App F 66.5/62.7/87.3; App H Table 8; App L P(intended); open-source rebuttal Cliff's delta = 1.0) must be treated as language-similarity, not persona expression. Decision consequence: App H's operational "recommended k" and the rebuttal's open-source claims are not evidence-grade.

**Q4 — Were personas behaviorally distinguishable at t=0?** Barely (V7). One effective behavioral dimension (ENTJ buys slightly more in days 1–5); ISFJ and INTJ statistically indistinguishable in early cash (p=0.356); all personas start ~87% cash regardless of mandate; no persona is ever near its C_ideal. Additional damning detail: static ENTJ's cash *declines toward its 0.2 target* over the run (Q4 cash 0.235) — the "drift" narrative reverses sign for one of three personas. The persona axis survives only as "prompt vocabulary modulates trading propensity somewhat"; the psychometric framing does not survive.

**Q5 — If effects don't track injected content, is the instruction-salience reframing acceptable/publishable?** The data are now in between the two framings, and I computed which parts belong to which: the **direction** of the behavioral effect (cash up) is content-independent (all three mandates raise cash: +25.9/+9.6/+5.9pp — so the "bidirectional" metric pattern would appear under any cash-raising injection); the **magnitude** is content-dependent in the right ordering (protective mandate ≫ growth mandate, despite less headroom), and the declarative placebo produces ~none of it (ISFJ placebo cash 5.0% vs memory 17.1% on Sonnet). Honest framing: *"Injected imperative directives shift stateless financial agents toward inaction, with magnitude ordered by directive semantics; scoring against opposed allocation targets converts this into an apparent bidirectional effect."* Venue tier for that, as-is: **findings-track / strong workshop**. What raises it a tier (COLM main): (i) swapped-mandate arm showing cash shift tracks injected content, not agent identity; (ii) an imperative-matched (directive) placebo + wrapper-only arm to kill the directive-force confound; (iii) model-level inference throughout (the per-persona sign results already pass it: p=1.4e-4 / 1.3e-3). All three are cheap (≈100–200 simulations).

**Q6 — Does the crash effect survive at model level under any defensible specification?** No (V2): sign 12/18 p=0.238; Wilcoxon-on-model-means p=0.119; t-test p=0.137; mixed-effects p=0.096–0.112. Direction is consistent (+3.1pp mean, and all three δ levels agree at pooled level) so the honest statement is "directionally suggestive, underpowered at the model level, not significant." The panic-selling failure mode as a *finding* must be withdrawn or downgraded to descriptive; as a *benchmark axis* (a scenario the suite offers) it may stay.

## 3. Red-team of the surviving contribution

**The claim under test (Cycle 1's sole survivor):** holding environment, position, cadence, model fixed, varying only mandate semantic content reverses the sign of the effect — 17/18 improve under capital preservation, 16/18 degrade under growth, identical market.

**Counts: real.** Reproduced exactly, and they pass model-level sign tests (the only headline numbers in the paper that do).

**Tautology objection — largely upheld, with a salvageable core.** The chain is verified end-to-end in the repo: (a) the ENTJ mandate literally says "If the trend is up, BUY. If the trend breaks, SELL" (`mbti_profiles.json`); in a flat market the trend is rarely up, so complying = not buying/selling out — which *raises* cash; (b) MAS = |mean cash − C_ideal| (`numerical_analysis.py:153-156`), and no MBTI prompt ever states the target (verified by regex); (c) the O3 ablation replaces the strategy text with "REMINDER: Your target cash allocation is 20%. Adhere to this target" (`ocean_prompts.py:215-217`) — i.e. it *tells the model the answer to the metric* — and the "harm" flips to a small benefit (−0.028) while O3-conservative pegs MAS=0.000 in both arms on all three models. So the O3 result, which the rebuttal presents as the anti-tautology defense, actually completes the tautology demonstration: when the injected text states the graded target, adherence is near-perfect; when it states a trading strategy, the model follows the strategy and is graded against an uncommunicated number. **"Re-grounding actively worsens behavior for aggressive agents" decodes to: the model correctly follows its injected conditional strategy, and the metric penalises that because the target is hidden and the market never triggers the BUY condition.** A sharp top-venue reviewer would reach this in one afternoon with the repo open. Can existing data discriminate LLM-finding from prompt-wording-finding? Partially — see below — but not decisively; the swapped-mandate arm is required.

**Cash-mediation — upheld with one important survivor.** B's conjecture is quantitatively correct in direction: the intervention's behavioral effect is a *unidirectional* cash increase (+25.9/+9.6/+5.9pp for ISFJ/ENTJ/INTJ), and the metric sign reversal is manufactured by oppositely-signed (C−C_ideal). Run-level corr(Δcash, ΔMAS) = −1.000 (ISFJ, exact identity) and +0.905 (ENTJ). **But** the effect is not one *uniform* shift: the protective mandate moves cash 2.7× more than the growth mandate despite the ISFJ arm having less headroom, and the declarative placebo moves it ~0. So content modulates magnitude even though it does not flip behavioral direction. That is the genuinely surviving empirical kernel.

**Degenerate-success audit (new):** 35.6% of ISFJ-memory flat runs execute **zero trades** over 200 days (vs 3.3% static); the five models the paper celebrates for "perfect adherence (MAS = 0.000)" (deepseek-chat, gemma-2-9b, gpt-4.1, gpt-4o, gpt-4o-mini) are models rendered completely inert by the injected instruction. "Perfect adherence" = agent does nothing for 200 days, indistinguishable from a refusal loop. This must be reported.

**Prior art (read from references/):**
- **Li et al. (Kim/Li) COLM 2024** (`mech_interp/Kim_Suzgun_2024_…`): System Prompt Repetition — inject the system prompt with probability p before each user turn — as a *baseline*, with an MMLU capability-cost calibration axis. Owns position/repetition/frequency (kills the paper's App H as a novelty and the "future work: frequency across suite" claim), but studies genuine multi-turn drift and never varies the *semantic content* of the injected instruction to produce opposite-signed behavioral effects.
- **ContextEcho 2605.24279**: 23 models, real 3.7k–9.7k-turn agentic-coding sessions, length-matched filler control, judge-free metrics, A-anchor intervention that restores register on *every* target. Owns long-horizon persona drift measurement and the filler-control design; no behavioral/financial task, no ground truth, no content-sign-reversal.
- **Meta Proactive Memory 2607.08716**: coins "behavioral state decay"; a memory agent decides *when* to inject; ablations of selective vs always-on. Owns the when-to-inject axis and the term the paper would need; does not vary injected-directive semantics in a fixed environment.
- **CLQT 2606.29771 §3.5**: ships a threshold-triggered re-grounding controller in a financial benchmark (ConsistencyScore Ct with an `atarget` "mandate alignment" component; "When Ct < 0.7 a drift warning is injected"). Owns adaptive re-grounding-in-finance; does not vary mandate content.
None of the four owns "content of the injected directive determines direction/magnitude of behavioral change in an identical market." The seam Cycle 1 identified is real — but the seam is narrower than Cycle 1 stated, because the *sign* half of the claim is metric-manufactured (V8).

**Verdict.** The contribution survives in this strongest defensible form and no stronger:
> *In a fixed synthetic market with stateless single-turn agents, appending an imperative behavioral directive to the prompt shifts trading behavior toward inaction/cash in a content-modulated way (protective ≫ growth ≫ neutral in magnitude, model-level p<0.002 for the two endpoint personas across 18 models), while a length- and position-matched declarative placebo produces no such shift; scoring this one behavioral shift against opposed allocation targets yields the observed 17/18 vs 16/18 split.*
As a "sign reversal of re-grounding" it does not survive; as a content-vs-position result with a candid account of the metric geometry it does. A top-venue reviewer would kill the current framing on the tautology; the reframed version with the swapped-mandate and directive-placebo arms would stand.
